CORE — one fund forever

  

  

    about CORE
    Broad, cheap, physical, accumulating UCITS funds, chosen broadest-first — all-world → developed →
    emerging → US → ex-US → Europe → Japan → Asia-Pacific — then domicile (IE first, for withholding),
    then cheapest TER, then largest AUM. One row per fund name, capped per
    sleeve so no index family crowds out the rest. This page then ranks the chosen funds like every table

    One all-world fund is already a whole

    and bury these at 0.0 — that is the ranking working, not a verdict on the fund.
    NOT advice.

    about this table
    S&P names whose English Wikipedia page gained the most readers: the median, over the last
    12 complete months, of each month's views over the same month a year before (VIEWS YoY). The

Berkshire holdings — shadow, NOT a buy signal

  

    about this table
    Every stock in Berkshire Hathaway's newest 13F, plus two kinds of row no 13F lists: a stake a Form 4
    net buy of the last 90 days opened (STATUS new (Form 4)) and its Japanese trading-house stakes,
    read from Japan's EDINET large-holding reports. Its wholly owned companies
    are not here. WEIGHT is the holding's share of the 13F's reported value, — for a row no 13F lists.
    STATUS compares it with the quarter before (a Japan stake with its report before): new, added (more
    than 5% more shares), trimmed (below 95% of the shares) or held (a forward split counts as held),
    and SHARES Δ is that change. BOUGHT is the trade date of a Form 4 buy, the day of a grown Japan
    stake's newest report (EDINET), the newest quarter it was bought in over the last 4, before the
    oldest of those quarters when it was bought in none of them, or —.
    A 13F is filed up to 45 days after its quarter ends, so the table is already weeks old. ETFs are
    left out. A name the screen does not price shows OpenFIGI's upper-case issuer name. A held stock ranks a little higher above
    (growth_brk_held_boost), and an uploaded settings.yaml does not change this table.

    about this table
    Stocks people say they are buying, one row per stock, from free sources. Superinvestors:
    Dataroma's last-quarter buys, kept when at least 8 of its ~80 tracked managers bought; these come

    13D/G: a new (not amended) SCHEDULE 13D or 13G stake those managers filed in the last 90 days, one
    buyer per filing; these are fresher than 13F and the row shows even when the screen does not price it.
    ARK: a stock an ARK fund (ARKK, ARKW, ARKG, ARKQ, ARKF, ARKX) bought in the last 30 days with no sale.
    Insiders: openinsider.com's latest cluster buys, two or more insiders buying on the open market.
    ARK and insider rows show only for stocks the screen prices.
    App Store: a company whose app climbed Apple's US top-free chart by 25 or more places over its
    best rank of the week before, or entered it that week; matched on the developer's name, and a
    developer with 3 or more charted apps (Google, Microsoft) is skipped. Silent for the first week of ranks.
    YouTube: stocks named in the newest uploads (about 15 each, roughly one week) of Dumb Money Live, a
    social-arbitrage channel, and of twelve more stock channels (Joseph Carlson, Ticker Symbol: YOU, Financial Education, Tom Nash, Everything Money, Learn to Invest, Chip Stock Investor, Dividendology, Meet Kevin, Stock Moe, Andrei Jikh and InvestAnswers); the first word of the stock's name must appear as a whole word in the same
    capitalisation, and a mention is not a buy (shorts and avoids are discussed too). Reddit: ApeWisdom's most-mentioned stocks, kept for a surge of 10 or more mentions in a day at 3 times the day before, or 50 or more whatever the day before (Reddit and 4chan). Google Trends: a US daily trending search whose whole title is the stock's ticker or name. Benzinga buzz: a ticker named in a Benzinga buzz article from the last 8 days. HN: Hacker News stories of the last 7 days with over 100 points that name the stock. Hand list: claims

    ranks a little higher above when a source other than the superinvestors names it (see the heading's

Young (listed under 5 years) — shadow, NOT a buy signal

  

    about this table
    Every stock the screen prices that has been listed for less than 5 years (YRS), including the big
    US IPOs of the last 5 years (an offer of $1 billion or more on Nasdaq's IPO calendar, SPACs left out).
    The rankings above need at least a 5-year price record, so a young stock cannot rank there however
    well it does; this table is where to watch it. Rows rank by SCORE and gates missed like the tables
    around it, so most read young or history in MISSES; REV-YoY is the newest full year's revenue growth. A short record is
    a real risk: a young stock has not yet been through a full cycle, and a fast-growing year can be a
    peak. Each run journals these names and track grades them against the bought book, to
    learn whether keeping young stocks out costs money. An uploaded settings.yaml does not change this table.
  

  


  
  
Braked out (pass every gate, held out by the brake) — shadow, NOT a buy signal

  

    about this table
    The Stocks table brakes a stock that has risen far above its own 200-week average: the further above,
    the lower its score, down to a small fraction at 100% above. A stock here passes every buy gate (MISSES 0),
    and its score with the brake switched off (NO-BRAKE) is at or above the lowest score in the Stocks table
    (TABLE-CUT), so the brake is the only thing keeping it out. BRAKE is the multiplier and the stretch it read.
    The brake stays because every easing of it tried so far lost money on history: a weaker brake, a higher
    floor, a floor for earnings-backed stocks and a trend-adjusted stretch were all refused. This table only
    shows what the brake costs today. An uploaded settings.yaml does not change it.

Bonds

  

  

    about the bond returns
    Each row is one government's 10-year bond, the benchmark safe asset of its market. A cell is the
    total return of holding it for that span, compounded: bought at par each month at that month's
    yield, sold a month later at the next, coupons reinvested. A negative cell is a real loss, not a
    bug: when yields rise, a bond you already hold falls in price, about 8% per point for a 10-year.
    They rose fast in 2021-23 (Germany's from about -0.5% to 3%) after years of near-zero coupons, so
    a span starting before that is still under water. Real bond funds lost the same. A span that starts
    at a low yield and then sees yields rise loses; one that starts high is carried by its coupons. Hover
    a cell for its % a year and the yield it was bought at. It is rebuilt from FRED's monthly
    average yields, so it is a model with no fees, not a fund you can buy. Each row is in its own
    currency; USA in EUR is the same Treasury for a euro holder, the dollar's swing included.
    — = the series is shorter (euro FX starts in 1999, Italy in 1991, Portugal in 1993); n/a = no data.
    Compare a cell with the Inflation table above to see the real return.

    tables are here so track can grade BEST on months that had not happened yet; hover any
    header or cell for its detail. NOT advice: nothing here places an order or changes BUY NOW.
