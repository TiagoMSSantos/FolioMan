CORE — one fund forever

  

  

    about CORE
    Broad, cheap, physical, accumulating UCITS funds, ranked broadest-first — all-world → developed →
    emerging → US → ex-US → Europe → Japan → Asia-Pacific — then domicile (IE first, for withholding),
    then cheapest TER, then largest AUM. One row per fund name, capped per
    sleeve so no index family crowds out the rest. One all-world fund is already a whole
    book; the sleeves under it are for building one yourself. The tables above rank momentum
    and bury these at 0.0 — that is the ranking working, not a verdict on the fund.
    NOT advice.
  


  
  
Attention — shadow, NOT a buy signal

  

    about this table
    S&P names whose English Wikipedia page gained the most readers: the median, over the last
    12 complete months, of each month's views over the same month a year before (VIEWS YoY). The
    pool is the 182 names the probe mapped to an article. Graded on history, this top third beat the
    pool by +14.7pp over 3 years and LOST −10.8pp over 5, so it reverses: it never feeds BUY% or the
    rankings above, and an uploaded settings.yaml does not change it. The 5-year read rests on less
    than one independent window (pageviews start in 2015), so neither the reversal nor any fix for it
    is settled yet.
  

  

  
  
Berkshire holdings — shadow, NOT a buy signal

  

    about this table
    Every stock in Berkshire Hathaway's newest 13F. A 13F lists US-listed holdings only, so its
    Japanese trading houses and its wholly owned companies are not here. WEIGHT is the holding's
    share of the 13F's reported value. STATUS compares it with the quarter before: new, added (more
    than 5% more shares), trimmed (below 95% of the shares) or held (a forward split counts as held),
    and SHARES Δ is that change. BOUGHT is the newest quarter it was bought in over the last 4, or —.
    A 13F is filed up to 45 days after its quarter ends, so the table is already weeks old. ETFs are
    left out. A name the screen does not price shows OpenFIGI's upper-case issuer name. Every holding
    is listed whatever the row count above. A held stock ranks a little higher above
    (growth_brk_held_boost), and an uploaded settings.yaml does not change this table.
  

  

  
  
Social Arbitrage trading — shadow, NOT a buy signal

  

    about this table
    Stocks people say they are buying, one row per stock, from free sources. Superinvestors:
    Dataroma's last-quarter buys, kept when at least 8 of its ~80 tracked managers bought; these come
    from 13F filings, so they are 45 or more days old, and a Berkshire buy above can show again here.
    When Dataroma does not answer, the same managers' own 13F filings on SEC EDGAR are counted instead
    (any share increase, a stock split excluded) and the source reads "superinvestors (SEC 13F) N".
    13D/G: a new (not amended) SCHEDULE 13D or 13G stake those managers filed in the last 90 days, one
    buyer per filing; these are fresher than 13F and the row shows even when the screen does not price it.
    ARK: a stock an ARK fund (ARKK, ARKW, ARKG, ARKQ, ARKF, ARKX) bought in the last 30 days with no sale.
    Insiders: openinsider.com's latest cluster buys, two or more insiders buying on the open market.
    ARK and insider rows show only for stocks the screen prices.
    App Store: a company whose app climbed Apple's US top-free chart by 25 or more places over its
    best rank of the week before, or entered it that week; matched on the developer's name, and a
    developer with 3 or more charted apps (Google, Microsoft) is skipped. Silent for the first week of ranks.
    YouTube: stocks named in the newest uploads (about 15 each, roughly one week) of Dumb Money Live, a
    social-arbitrage channel, and of Joseph Carlson, Ticker Symbol: YOU, Financial Education and Tom Nash; the first word of the stock's name must appear as a whole word in the same
    capitalisation, and a mention is not a buy (shorts and avoids are discussed too). Hand list: claims
    checked against a linked source, kept in
    src/buy-heuristics/social-arbitrage-trading.json. SOURCES names each source that lists
    the stock; NEWEST is the latest dated mention, or the 13F quarter; WHY quotes the hand list, else
    the newest video title, else the newest filing. A NAME of — means the screen does not price it. The
    rows rank a little higher above (see the heading's hover), and an uploaded settings.yaml does not
    change this table.
  

  

  
  
Young (listed under 5 years) — shadow, NOT a buy signal

  

    about this table
    Every stock the screen prices that has been listed for less than 5 years (YRS), including the big
    US IPOs of the last 5 years (an offer of $1 billion or more on Nasdaq's IPO calendar, SPACs left out).
    The rankings above need at least a 5-year price record, so a young stock cannot rank there however
    well it does; this table is where to watch it. Rows sort by REV-YoY, the newest full year's revenue
    growth, fastest first; a stock with no revenue figure is listed last, not dropped. A short record is
    a real risk: a young stock has not yet been through a full cycle, and a fast-growing year can be a
    peak. Each run journals these names and track grades them against the bought book, to
    learn whether keeping young stocks out costs money. An uploaded settings.yaml does not change this table.

Bonds

  

  

    about the bond returns
    Each row is one government's 10-year bond, the benchmark safe asset of its market. A cell is the
    total return of holding it for that span, compounded: bought at par each month at that month's
    yield, sold a month later at the next, coupons reinvested. A negative cell is a real loss, not a
    bug: when yields rise, a bond you already hold falls in price, about 8% per point for a 10-year.
    They rose fast in 2021-23 (Germany's from about -0.5% to 3%) after years of near-zero coupons, so
    a span starting before that is still under water. Real bond funds lost the same. It is rebuilt from FRED's monthly
    average yields, so it is a model with no fees, not a fund you can buy. Each row is in its own
    currency; USA in EUR is the same Treasury for a euro holder, the dollar's swing included.
    n/a = the series is shorter (euro FX starts in 1999, Italy in 1991, Portugal in 1993).
    Compare a cell with the Inflation table above to see the real return.
  


  

    about the rankings
    screen's three ranked tables — the 20yr+ growth ranking:
    at/near its own ~10y high with a strong proven long-term CAGR and an accelerating recent year,
    braked by how far it has run above its 200wk trend. Crypto is ranked against Bitcoin, the market
    base. These ranked tilts are higher-variance, regime-dependent sector/thematic bets whose recent
    leadership won't necessarily repeat. NOT advice.
  

  
  

    which ranking this is
    Which ranking this is. These rows come from
    tests/ci-settings.yaml — the same configuration the repo's own test suite grades, with
    no overlay of any kind. Every gate and weight behind them carries a written receipt in that file
    naming what was measured and at which horizons. That is a statement about provenance, not
    about outcome: the receipts were measured on historical windows, several were fitted before the
    point-in-time universe landed and are marked PRE-PIT because that fitting is now
    suspect, and a live universe is not the backtest pool. A ranking you can audit is still a ranking.
