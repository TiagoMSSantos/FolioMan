CORE — one fund forever

  

  

    about CORE
    Broad, cheap index funds sold under EU fund rules (UCITS) that own the real shares (physical) and
    reinvest the dividends (accumulating). They are chosen broadest first: all-world, then developed,
    emerging, US, ex-US, Europe, Japan and Asia-Pacific. Then by home country (Ireland first: it pays less
    tax on US dividends), then the lowest yearly fee (TER), then the largest size
    (AUM). One row per fund, and each group of funds (a sleeve) has a cap, so no single
    index crowds out the rest. This page ranks the chosen funds by buy score and buy rules missed, like
    every table below it (hover the headers). One all-world fund is already a whole
    portfolio; the groups under it are for building one yourself. The tables above rank recent
    momentum, so they score these funds 0. That is how that ranking works, not a verdict on the fund.
    NOT advice.

Attention — watch only, NOT a buy signal

  

    about this table
    S&P 500 companies whose English Wikipedia page gained the most readers. VIEWS YoY compares each
    month's views with the same month a year before; the table shows the middle value of the last 12 full
    months. It covers the 182 companies matched to a Wikipedia article. On past prices, this top third
    beat all 182 by 14.7 points over 3 years. Over 5 years it LOST by 10.8 points, so the signal flips.
    That is why it never changes BUY% or the rankings above, and your own settings file does not change
    it. Wikipedia counts start in 2015, so the 5-year result rests on less than one full test period.
    Neither the flip nor any fix for it is settled yet.
  

  

  
  
Berkshire holdings — watch only, NOT a buy signal

  

    about this table
    Every stock in Berkshire Hathaway's newest 13F, the quarterly holdings report big US investors file.
    Two kinds of row come from elsewhere. One is a stake a net buy opened in the last 90 days. That buy is filed on Form 4, the report a big holder files when it trades. Its STATUS reads new (Form 4). The other is its
    Japanese trading-house stakes, read from Japan's large-holding reports (EDINET). The companies
    Berkshire owns outright are not here. WEIGHT is the holding's share of the 13F's total value, or —
    for a row no 13F lists. STATUS compares it with the quarter before (a Japan stake with its report
    before). It reads new, added (over 5% more shares), trimmed (under 95% of the shares) or held; a
    stock split counts as held. SHARES Δ is that change. BOUGHT is the trade date of a Form 4 buy, or
    the day of a grown Japan stake's newest report. Otherwise it is the newest of the last 4 quarters it was bought in. It reads "before" the oldest of those quarters when it was bought in none of them, or —.
    A 13F is filed up to 45 days after its quarter ends, so the table is already weeks old. Funds (ETFs)
    are left out. A name this page does not price shows its issuer name in capitals, as OpenFIGI (a free
    ticker lookup) lists it. A stock Berkshire holds ranks a little higher in the tables above. Your own
    settings file does not change this table.
  

  

  
  
Social Arbitrage trading — watch only, NOT a buy signal

  

    about this table
    Stocks people say they are buying, one row per stock, from free sources. Superinvestors: last
    quarter's buys from a site that follows about 80 famous fund managers, kept when at least 8 of them
    bought. These come from 13F filings (the quarterly holdings report big US investors file), so they
    are 45 or more days old. A Berkshire buy above can show again here. When that site does not answer,
    the same managers' own 13F filings at the US regulator (SEC EDGAR) are counted instead. Then any rise
    in shares counts, a stock split excluded, and the source reads "superinvestors (SEC 13F) N".
    13D/G: a new (not amended) big-stake filing (SCHEDULE 13D or 13G) by those managers in the last 90
    days, one buyer per filing. These are fresher than a 13F, and the row shows even when this page does
    not price the stock. ARK: a stock an ARK fund (ARKK, ARKW, ARKG, ARKQ, ARKF, ARKX) bought in the last
    30 days with no sale. Insiders: openinsider.com's latest cluster buys, two or more of the company's
    own bosses or directors buying on the open market. ARK and insider rows show only for stocks this
    page prices. App Store: a company whose app climbed Apple's US top-free chart by 25 or more places, or entered it that week. The climb is against its best rank of the week before. It is matched on the developer's
    name, and a developer with 3 or more charted apps (Google, Microsoft) is skipped. Silent for the
    first week of ranks. YouTube: stocks named in the newest uploads (about 15 each, roughly one week) of
    13 stock channels. They are Dumb Money Live, a social-arbitrage channel, and Joseph Carlson; Ticker
    Symbol: YOU; Financial Education; Tom Nash; Everything Money; Learn to Invest; Chip Stock Investor;
    Dividendology; Meet Kevin; Stock Moe; Andrei Jikh; and InvestAnswers. The first word of the stock's
    name must appear as a whole word, in the same capitals. A mention is not a buy: shorts and stocks
    to avoid are discussed too. Reddit: ApeWisdom's most-mentioned stocks on Reddit and 4chan. A stock is kept for 10 or more mentions in a day at 3 times the day before. It is also kept for 50 or more, whatever the day before. Google Trends: a US daily trending search whose whole title is the stock's
    ticker or name. Benzinga buzz: a ticker named in a Benzinga buzz article from the last 8 days. HN:
    Hacker News stories of the last 7 days with over 100 points that name the stock. Hand list: claims
    checked against a linked source and kept by hand. SOURCES names each source that lists the stock;
    NEWEST is the latest dated mention, or the 13F quarter. WHY quotes the hand list, else the newest
    video title, else the newest filing. A NAME of — means this page does not price it. A row ranks a
    little higher in the tables above when a source other than the superinvestors names it. Your own
    settings file does not change this table.

Ran too far (pass every buy rule, held out by the ran-too-far cut) — watch only, NOT a buy signal

  

    about this table
    The Stocks table cuts the score of a stock that has risen far above its own 200-week (about 4-year)
    average price. The further above, the lower its score, down to a small fraction at 100% above. A
    stock here passes every buy rule (MISSES 0). Its score without the cut (NO-BRAKE) is at or above the lowest score in the Stocks table (TABLE-CUT). So the cut is the only thing keeping it out. BRAKE is
    the multiplier, and how far above the average the stock was. The cut stays because every softer
    version tried so far lost money on past prices. A weaker cut, a higher minimum, a minimum for
    stocks backed by earnings and a trend-adjusted distance were all refused. This table only shows what
    the cut costs today. Your own settings file does not change it.

Inflation

  

  

    about inflation and the column units
    How much prices rose over the same spans: USA, the EU (HICP) and Portugal. Return columns of 1 year
    and longer, on this run: (unit not stated). A before inflation
    column still holds inflation. In today's money it is (1 + column) ÷ (1 + row) − 1. For example, +50% over 5 years with 20% inflation is about +25% after it. An after inflation column already
    had the EU row taken out, so do not take it out again. 1D/1W/1M are always before inflation.
  

  
  
Bonds

  

  

    about the bond returns
    Each row is one government's 10-year bond, the usual safe asset of its market. A cell is the total
    return of holding it for that span, compounded. The model buys a new bond each month at that month's yield (its interest rate). It sells it a month later at the next, and reinvests the interest. A negative
    cell is a real loss, not a bug. When rates rise, a bond you already hold falls in price: about 8%
    for each 1-point rise, for a 10-year bond. Rates rose fast in 2021-23 (Germany's from about -0.5% to
    3%) after years of near-zero interest. So a span starting before that is still under water, and real
    bond funds lost the same. A span that starts at a low rate and then sees rates rise loses; one that
    starts high is carried by its interest. Hover a cell for its % a year and the rate it was bought at.
    It is rebuilt from monthly average rates on FRED (the St. Louis Fed's free data site), so it is a
    model with no fees, not a fund you can buy. Each row is in its own currency; USA in EUR is
    the same US bond for a euro holder, the dollar's swing included. — = the series is shorter (euro
    exchange rates start in 1999, Italy in 1991, Portugal in 1993); n/a = no data. Compare a cell with
    the Inflation table above to see the return after inflation.

    about these tables
    The tests on past prices agree: after costs, buying and holding beat every way of trading in and out
    they measured. The one line that passes, Lifecycle 200/50 in the table above, is a way to save, not
    to trade. The two tables below price each name four ways over its last 2 years. Each trade is €1,000, in euros at each day's EUR/USD rate, after costs. BEST names the way that made the most, which is

    any header or cell for its detail. NOT advice: nothing here places an order or changes the buy list.

    about the rankings
    The three ranked tables (Stocks, ETFs, Crypto) rank for a hold of 20 years or more. A top name sits at or near its own high of about 10 years. It has strong proven growth per year over the long run, and a faster recent year. Its score is cut the further it has run above its 200-week (about 4-year)

    which ranking this is
    Which ranking this is. These rows come from one settings file,
    tests/ci-settings.yaml, the same one the project's own tests check, with nothing added
    on top. Every buy rule and weight in it carries a written note of what was measured and over which
    spans. That says where the numbers come from, not how they will do. The notes were measured on past
    prices. Several were tuned before the tests used each past day's own list of companies, not just
    today's survivors. They are marked PRE-PIT (before that change) because that tuning is
    now suspect. And today's companies are not the ones the tests used. A ranking you can check is still
    only a ranking.
